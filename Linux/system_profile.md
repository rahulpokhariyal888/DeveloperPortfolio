## Binary Number Representation

### 1. Decimal to Binary Conversion (75)

To convert decimal 75 into binary, repeatedly divide the number by 2
and record the remainder.

| Division | Quotient | Remainder |
|---|---:|---:|
| 75 ÷ 2 | 37 | 1 |
| 37 ÷ 2 | 18 | 1 |
| 18 ÷ 2 | 9 | 0 |
| 9 ÷ 2 | 4 | 1 |
| 4 ÷ 2 | 2 | 0 |
| 2 ÷ 2 | 1 | 0 |
| 1 ÷ 2 | 0 | 1 |

Reading the remainders from bottom to top:

**75 (Decimal) = 1001011 (Binary)**

### 2. Binary to Decimal Conversion (110101)

Using the positional weight method:

Binary number: 110101

| Binary Digit | Positional Weight | Calculation |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2^5 = 32 | 1 × 32 = 32 |
| 1 | 2^4 = 16 | 1 × 16 = 16 |
| 0 | 2^3 = 8 | 0 × 8 = 0 |
| 1 | 2^2 = 4 | 1 × 4 = 4 |
| 0 | 2^1 = 2 | 0 × 2 = 0 |
| 1 | 2^0 = 1 | 1 × 1 = 1 |

### 3. Why Computers Use Binary

Computers use binary internally because digital electronic circuits
can reliably represent two states, ON (1) and OFF (0), making
binary an efficient and dependable way to process and store data.
